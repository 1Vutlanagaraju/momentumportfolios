How Can I Cut Losses in Intraday Trading in the Stock Market?

Successful intraday trading is not about avoiding every loss. It is about controlling losses before they become dangerous.

To cut losses, the first step is to understand why losses happen. Many traders focus only on finding the next winning stock or strategy, but the foundation of intraday trading is actually risk management.

The objective should not be to win every trade. The objective should be to keep losing trades small while allowing profitable trades enough room to grow.

1. Understand Position Size and Quantity

One of the major reasons traders lose money is that they enter trades without understanding position sizing and quantity distribution.

Many traders simply open their trading application, select a stock, and buy whatever quantity they feel like buying.

Ask yourself:
Why am I buying this particular quantity? How much money am I willing to lose if the trade goes wrong?

If you cannot answer these questions before entering the trade, your position size may be based more on emotion than on risk management.

Many traders enter the market expecting to become wealthy very quickly. This mindset can encourage excessive position sizes and unnecessary risk.

Therefore, before entering a trade, define your rules in advance: entry price, stop-loss, quantity, maximum acceptable loss, and exit conditions.

2. Don't Cut Profits Too Early and Don't Average a Losing Trade Blindly

Another common problem among intraday traders is the tendency to book small profits quickly while allowing losses to become larger.

For example, when a trader sees an MTM profit of ₹1,000, they may immediately close the position because they are afraid that the profit will disappear.

Later, the stock may continue moving in the expected direction, and the trader realizes that waiting according to a predefined exit strategy could have resulted in a larger profit.

The opposite problem is even more dangerous. When the stock starts moving against their prediction, some traders start adding more quantity and averaging down without a predefined plan.

They may also keep moving their stop-loss further away just to avoid accepting that the original trade idea was wrong.

A small planned loss can be manageable. An uncontrolled loss can become a serious problem.

3. Never Treat Stop-Loss as Optional

There are also traders who do not use a stop-loss at all.

In volatile markets, this can expose a trading account to losses much larger than originally intended.

A stop-loss does not guarantee that the exact exit price will always be achieved, particularly during sharp price movements or gaps. However, having a predefined exit level can help traders control risk and avoid making emotional decisions during a rapidly moving market.

Why this matters

A sudden market move can result in a large loss within minutes. The faster the market moves, the less time a trader may have to react emotionally and make a rational decision.

A Simple Intraday Risk-Management Checklist

✓ Define your entry before placing the trade.
✓ Decide your stop-loss before entering.
✓ Calculate position size based on your risk.
✓ Never increase quantity simply because a trade is losing.
✓ Avoid moving your stop-loss farther away just to avoid booking a loss.
✓ Have a predefined profit-taking or trailing-stop strategy.
✓ Protect your trading capital before thinking about maximizing profits.

Final Thoughts

Intraday trading is not about being right on every trade. Even experienced traders have losing trades.

The difference is often in how they manage those losses. A trader who controls position size, follows predefined risk rules, respects stop-loss levels, and avoids emotional averaging has a framework for managing downside risk.

“Cut the losses. Protect the capital. Let profitable trades breathe.”
